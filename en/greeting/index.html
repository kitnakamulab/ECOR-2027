Chair’s Message / 大会長挨拶

The 1st International Conference on Extracorporeal Organ Regeneration (ECOR2027)

Conference ChairMakoto NakamuraProfessor, Kanazawa Institute of Technology

We are pleased to announce that the 1st International Conference on Extracorporeal Organ Regeneration (ECOR) will take place at the Shiinoki Cultural Complex in Kanazawa on March 27–28, 2027.

After graduating from medical school, I trained in clinical medicine as a pediatrician in the Department of Pediatrics at Kanazawa University. During ten years of pediatric practice, I cared for a patient with a severe disease for which heart transplantation was the only effective treatment. That experience led me to medical research, first into artificial hearts and subsequently into regenerative biomedical engineering.

In regenerative biomedical engineering, I pursued the question, “Can we create organs using machines?” and worked to open up bioprinting as a new approach and field in regenerative medicine. Today, I am taking the next step toward growing and regenerating organs outside the body.

In April 2023, I returned to Kanazawa as a professor in the Department of Mechanical Engineering at Kanazawa Institute of Technology. Through collaboration between Kanazawa Institute of Technology and Kanazawa Medical University, I met Professor Kengo Furuichi, a nephrologist. I also had the opportunity to meet Professor Shintaro Yagi of Kanazawa University, who leads research on ex vivo liver perfusion. These encounters inspired me to initiate this international conference on extracorporeal organ regeneration.

Research aimed at treating and regenerating organs while maintaining their function through ex vivo perfusion represents an emerging area at the intersection of transplantation medicine and regenerative medicine. It holds the potential to open new possibilities for both fields. Achieving this goal will require connecting organ perfusion technologies with rapidly advancing stem cell science, regenerative therapies, and gene therapies, while bringing together new engineering technologies for organ monitoring and functional assessment, digitalization, automation, and AI. I believe that, by combining these capabilities, organ regeneration that is currently considered impossible can eventually become a reality.

Extracorporeal organ regeneration is an important theme for taking the research I have pursued throughout my career into its next stage and, ultimately, bringing regenerative treatments to people who are waiting for them.

At this conference, we will welcome leading researchers from Japan and abroad who have deep expertise in this field. Together with researchers in medicine and engineering, healthcare professionals, industry engineers, business professionals, and students, we hope to share a vision for extracorporeal organ regeneration and discuss the current state of the field, its challenges, and pathways for translating research into clinical practice. I hope that dialogue across disciplines and professional roles will foster new collaborations and propel the field forward.

I sincerely look forward to welcoming you to ECOR 2027.

Chair, ECOR 2027
Makoto Nakamura
